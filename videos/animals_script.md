# 10 Animals With REAL Superpowers

## HOOK (cold open)
There's a shrimp that punches as fast as a bullet. A frog that freezes solid every winter, its heart completely stopped, and then thaws out in spring and hops away. A bird that flies the distance of three trips to the Moon and back. And number one has three hearts, blue blood, and arms that can think for themselves. These are ten animals with real superpowers.

## #10 — THE HEART THAT NEVER RESTS (HUMMINGBIRDS)
Number ten: the hummingbird. Its heart can beat more than 1,200 times a minute, and its wings can flap around 80 times a second. It's the only bird that can fly backwards, and even upside down. But burning that much energy is dangerous. So at night, many hummingbirds enter a state called torpor: their body temperature plummets, and their heartbeat drops to as little as 50 beats a minute. Every night, they basically switch themselves off.

## #9 — THE LIVING MISSILE (PEREGRINE FALCON)
Number nine: the peregrine falcon, the fastest animal on the planet. When it dives on prey, it has been measured at around 390 kilometres an hour. At that speed, the air rushing in could damage its lungs, so it has small bony cones in its nostrils that slow the air down. Engineers used the same idea in the air intakes of early jet engines. And a see-through third eyelid protects its eyes, like built-in goggles.

## #8 — THE ENDLESS SUMMER (ARCTIC TERN)
Number eight: the Arctic tern. Every year it flies from the Arctic to Antarctica and back, chasing summer at both ends of the Earth. Tracking studies found some birds cover around 70,000 kilometres in a single year. Over a 30-year lifetime, that adds up to roughly 2.4 million kilometres: about three round trips to the Moon. And because it lives in two summers, it probably sees more daylight than any other animal.

## #7 — THE LIVING BATTERY (ELECTRIC EEL)
Number seven: the electric eel. Which, fun fact, isn't actually an eel. It's a type of knifefish. Its body is packed with thousands of cells called electrocytes that work like stacked batteries. In 2019, scientists described a species that can discharge up to 860 volts, the strongest electric shock of any animal known. And when they feel threatened, they can leap out of the water to shock whatever's attacking them.

## #6 — THE FROG THAT FREEZES (WOOD FROG · NORTH AMERICA)
Number six: the wood frog. When winter comes, it doesn't hide from the cold. It freezes. Up to around two thirds of the water in its body turns to ice. Its heart stops beating. It stops breathing. By every normal measure, it's dead. The secret is sugar: its liver floods its cells with glucose that works like antifreeze, protecting them from damage. Then, in spring, it thaws out... its heart starts again... and it hops away.

## #5 — THE ANIMAL THAT BREAKS THE RULES (PLATYPUS · AUSTRALIA)
Number five: the platypus. A mammal that lays eggs. The males have venomous spurs on their back legs. It has no stomach. Its bill can sense the tiny electric fields made by the muscles of its prey, so it hunts with its eyes, ears and nose closed. And in 2020, scientists discovered that under ultraviolet light, its fur glows a blue-green colour. When the first specimen reached Britain, some scientists thought it was a hoax.

## #4 — THE REAL-LIFE WOLVERINE (AXOLOTL · MEXICO)
Number four: the axolotl. Lose a leg? It grows a new one. It can also regrow parts of its spinal cord, its heart, and even its brain, with no scars. Scientists all over the world study it, hoping to unlock the same ability in humans. And it never really grows up: it keeps its baby features, like those feathery gills, its whole life. Sadly, in the wild, it survives almost only in the canals of Mexico City, and it's critically endangered.

## #3 — THE SHRIMP WITH A GUN (PISTOL SHRIMP)
Number three: the pistol shrimp. It's only a few centimetres long, but it has one oversized claw that snaps shut so fast it fires a jet of water that creates a bubble. When that bubble collapses, it releases a blast of sound louder than a gunshot, a tiny flash of light, and for a split second, temperatures of around 4,700 degrees. Almost as hot as the surface of the Sun. Colonies of them snap so loudly they can interfere with submarine sonar.

## #2 — THE BULLET PUNCH (MANTIS SHRIMP)
Number two: the mantis shrimp. Its club-like arms strike with the acceleration of a small-calibre bullet, more than 10,000 times the force of gravity. They hit so hard they can crack crab shells, snail shells... and even aquarium glass. Like the pistol shrimp, the punch is so fast it boils the water around it, so each target gets hit twice: once by the club, and once by the collapsing bubble. And its eyes have up to 16 types of colour receptors. We have three.

## #1 — THE ALIEN OF EARTH (OCTOPUS)
And number one: the octopus. It has three hearts and blue blood. It has around 500 million neurons, and about two thirds of them aren't in its head. They're in its arms. Each arm can taste, touch and make decisions on its own. It can change the colour and texture of its skin in a fraction of a second, to vanish against coral, sand or rock. And here's the twist: as far as scientists can tell, octopuses are colour-blind. Nobody fully understands how they match colours they may not be able to see. If aliens are real, they might look a lot like this.
