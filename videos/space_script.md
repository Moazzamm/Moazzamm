# 10 Terrifying Things Hiding in Space

## HOOK (cold open)
On Friday the 13th, in April 2029, an asteroid longer than three football fields will fly past Earth closer than our TV satellites. There's a planet where it rains glass, sideways, at seven times the speed of sound. And somewhere beyond our galaxy, something enormous is pulling the entire Milky Way toward it at over two million kilometres an hour. We can't see what it is. Here are ten terrifying things hiding in space.

## #10 — THE PLANET THAT RAINS GLASS (HD 189733 b · 63 LIGHT-YEARS AWAY)
Number ten. From far away, HD 189733 b looks like a beautiful blue marble, a lot like Earth. Up close, it's a nightmare. It's a gas giant orbiting so close to its star that the winds reach around 8,700 kilometres an hour. And its blue colour comes from clouds full of silicate particles. In other words, it rains glass. Sideways. If you stood there, you'd be shredded in seconds.

## #9 — THE PLANET THAT RAINS IRON (WASP-76 b · ~640 LIGHT-YEARS AWAY)
Number nine. On WASP-76 b, one side always faces its star, and it's so hot, around 2,400 degrees, that iron boils into vapour. Ferocious winds carry that metal vapour around to the cooler night side... where it condenses and falls as rain. Molten iron rain. Every evening on this planet is a storm of liquid metal.

## #8 — PLANETS WITH NO SUN (THE MILKY WAY)
Number eight. Not every planet has a sun. Rogue planets are worlds that were flung out of their solar systems, and now drift through the galaxy alone, in total darkness, forever. They're almost impossible to see, but astronomers now think there could be more of them than there are stars in the Milky Way. Which means one could, in theory, be drifting toward us right now... and we'd barely see it coming.

## #7 — THE HEAVIEST SUGAR CUBE (NEUTRON STARS)
Number seven. When a giant star dies, its core can collapse into a neutron star: a ball about the size of a city, with more mass than our Sun. It's so dense that a sugar cube of its material would weigh around a billion tonnes on Earth. About as much as a mountain. And some neutron stars spin more than 700 times every second.

## #6 — THE MAGNETAR (THE MOST MAGNETIC THING KNOWN)
Number six. Some neutron stars are even scarier. Magnetars have the strongest magnetic fields in the known universe, around a thousand trillion times stronger than Earth's. According to NASA, if a magnetar were placed halfway between Earth and the Moon, it would wipe the data from every credit card on the planet. Get closer, and the field would distort the atoms in your body.

## #5 — THE JUNKYARD ABOVE US (EARTH ORBIT)
Number five. We've turned the space around Earth into a junkyard. Agencies now track tens of thousands of pieces of debris, and there are estimated to be over a million fragments too small to track. They fly at around 28,000 kilometres an hour, roughly ten times faster than a bullet. In 2016, a tiny fleck of paint or metal chipped a window on the International Space Station. The nightmare scenario has a name: Kessler syndrome. One collision creates debris, which causes more collisions... until parts of orbit become unusable.

## #4 — FRIDAY THE 13TH, 2029 (ASTEROID 99942 APOPHIS)
In 2004, astronomers spotted an asteroid called Apophis, named after the Egyptian god of chaos. Early calculations gave it about a 2.7% chance of hitting Earth in 2029: the highest risk ever recorded at the time. Newer observations ruled out an impact. But on April 13th, 2029, a Friday the 13th, it will pass just 32,000 kilometres above us. Closer than the satellites that beam TV to your home. Over a billion people may be able to see it with their own eyes.

## #3 — THE GREAT NOTHING (THE BOÖTES VOID)
Number three. In 1981, astronomers found a hole in the universe. The Boötes Void is a region about 330 million light-years across that is almost completely empty. Where there should be thousands of galaxies, there are only around sixty. Astronomers like to say that if the Milky Way sat in the middle of it, we wouldn't have known other galaxies even existed until the 1960s.

## #2 — THE BIGGEST EXPLOSIONS EVER (GAMMA-RAY BURSTS)
Number two. Gamma-ray bursts are the most powerful explosions in the universe. In a few seconds, one can release more energy than our Sun will in its entire ten-billion-year life. In October 2022, the brightest burst ever recorded hit Earth from about two billion light-years away. It was so powerful it disturbed our upper atmosphere. Some scientists suspect a burst much closer to home may have helped trigger a mass extinction 450 million years ago.

## #1 — THE GREAT ATTRACTOR (BEHIND THE MILKY WAY)
And number one. Right now, you're moving. Not just around the Sun, but with our whole galaxy, at roughly two million kilometres an hour, toward something astronomers call the Great Attractor. It's a gravitational anomaly about 150 to 250 million light-years away, pulling on thousands of galaxies. And the most unsettling part? We can't get a clear look at it. It sits directly behind the dusty disk of our own Milky Way, in a region astronomers call the Zone of Avoidance. Something huge is pulling on us... and our own galaxy is blocking the view.
