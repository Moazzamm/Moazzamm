# The Congo Is Hiding Things Science Can't Explain

## HOOK (cold open)
There's a rainforest in Africa bigger than Mexico. Hidden inside it is a river so deep it could swallow a sixty-storey skyscraper... a lake sitting on enough gas to threaten two million people... and a village that gets hit by lightning more than almost anywhere on Earth. But the strangest thing in the Congo isn't any of those. It's something that has killed thousands of people. And fifty years later, scientists still don't know where it hides. These are the ten darkest secrets of the Congo.

## #10 — THE BOTTOMLESS RIVER (CONGO RIVER · DR CONGO)
Number ten. The Congo River is the deepest river ever measured. More than 220 meters in places. Deep enough to sink a sixty-storey skyscraper without a trace. It's also the only major river on Earth that crosses the equator twice. But the craziest part is what lives in its rapids. The current is so violent it acts like an underwater wall, splitting fish into separate species only a short swim apart. And in the deepest rapids, scientists found a ghost-white fish with no working eyes. A cave fish... living in a river.

## #9 — THE AFRICAN UNICORN (ITURI RAINFOREST · DR CONGO)
Number nine. For years, European explorers heard stories of an 'African unicorn', and dismissed it as a myth. Then, in 1901, they got proof. The okapi. It has the striped legs of a zebra, the body of a horse... and it's actually the closest living relative of the giraffe. It lives wild in only one country on Earth: the Democratic Republic of the Congo. And its tongue is so long, it can lick its own eyelids and clean its own ears.

## #8 — THE LIGHTNING VILLAGE (KIFUKA · EASTERN DR CONGO)
Number eight. Imagine living somewhere the sky itself attacks you. High in the mountains of eastern Congo is a village called Kifuka. NASA satellites found it's hit by about 158 lightning strikes per square kilometre, every single year. For years, that made it the most lightning-struck place on the planet. Warm, wet air from the rainforest slams into the mountains and builds monster thunderstorms, night after night. Only one place has since taken its crown: a lake in Venezuela.

## #7 — THE PEACEFUL APE (SOUTH OF THE CONGO RIVER)
Number seven. On the south bank of the Congo River lives an ape that shares around 98.7% of its DNA with us, and most people have never heard of it. The bonobo. More than a million years ago, the river split one ape population in two. North of it, chimpanzees evolved to be aggressive, with males in charge. South of it, bonobos built a society led by females, where conflict is often settled with affection instead of violence. Same ancestor. One river. Two completely different worlds.

## #6 — THE FASTEST LAVA ON EARTH (MOUNT NYIRAGONGO · DR CONGO)
Number six. Mount Nyiragongo holds one of the largest lava lakes on the planet. In 1977, its crater wall cracked, and the lava lake drained in less than an hour. Its lava is so runny that it raced downhill at up to 60 kilometres an hour. Faster than you can run. Today, more than a million people live in the city of Goma, right at its feet. In 2002, lava cut straight through the city. In 2021, it stopped just short of it.

## #5 — THE CARBON BOMB (CUVETTE CENTRALE · CONGO BASIN)
Number five. In 2017, scientists announced a discovery that had been hiding in plain sight. Beneath the swamp forests of the central Congo lies the largest tropical peatland on Earth: a soggy layer of dead plants that built up over thousands of years, covering an area bigger than England. It locks away around 30 billion tonnes of carbon. That's roughly what the entire world emits from fossil fuels in three years. If it ever dries out... all of that goes straight into the sky.

## #4 — THE LION-KILLER APES (BILI FOREST · NORTHERN DR CONGO)
Number four. For decades, hunters in northern Congo told stories of giant apes that killed lions. Skulls collected there looked part chimp, part gorilla. When researchers finally reached the Bili forest, they expected a new species. But the DNA said chimpanzee. And yet, these chimps don't act like any others. They often sleep in nests on the forest floor, where leopards hunt. Something other chimpanzees almost never risk. Why they're so bold, nobody fully knows.

## #3 — THE LAKE THAT COULD EXPLODE (LAKE KIVU · DR CONGO / RWANDA)
Lake Kivu looks peaceful. But dissolved in its deep water are an estimated 60 cubic kilometres of methane, and around 300 of carbon dioxide. If that deep water ever overturned, the gas could burst out all at once. It's called a limnic eruption, and it's happened before. In 1986, Lake Nyos in Cameroon released a cloud of carbon dioxide that suffocated more than 1,700 people in a single night. Lake Kivu is over a thousand times bigger. And around two million people live around it. The twist? Rwanda now pumps up that methane... and turns it into electricity.

## #2 — WHERE A PANDEMIC BEGAN (KINSHASA · DR CONGO)
Number two. In 2014, scientists traced the family tree of HIV's most widespread strain back to one place: Kinshasa, around the year 1920. The virus had crossed from chimpanzees to humans in the forests of southern Cameroon. Then it travelled to a booming colonial city. From there, railways and river boats carried it across Africa, and eventually, the world. Since then, it has taken more than 40 million lives.

## #1 — THE KILLER THAT VANISHES (YAMBUKU · NORTHERN DR CONGO)
And number one. In 1976, a mysterious fever tore through the village of Yambuku in northern Congo. Doctors named the virus after a nearby river: Ebola. Since then, it has killed around 15,000 people. Outbreaks are believed to start the same way. One person, deep in the forest, in contact with an infected animal. Fruit bats are the prime suspect. But here's the chilling part. After fifty years of searching, scientists have never confirmed where Ebola actually hides between outbreaks. It disappears without a trace... and then, it comes back.
