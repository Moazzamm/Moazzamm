# 10 Things That Look ALIEN Under a Microscope

## HOOK (cold open)
Everything around you is hiding a second world. Your bed is a feeding ground. Your face has residents that come out at night. And one creature you could find in a puddle has survived the vacuum of outer space. But the most alien thing you'll see in this video isn't out there. It's you. Let's zoom in.

## #10 — SAND (MAGNIFIED ~50×)
Number ten: sand. Put a pinch of beach sand under a microscope, and it stops looking like dirt and starts looking like treasure. Tiny spiral shells, star-shaped skeletons, polished crystals. Many of those grains were once living creatures. And on tropical beaches, there's an even weirder source: parrotfish. They bite off chunks of coral, grind it up... and poop it out as fine white sand. A single parrotfish can make hundreds of kilos of sand every year. So that perfect white beach? A lot of it is fish poop.

## #9 — THE BLUE THAT ISN'T BLUE (BLUE MORPHO BUTTERFLY)
Number nine. This butterfly is one of the bluest things in nature. But zoom in on its wing, and there's a problem. There is no blue in it. The wing is covered in thousands of tiny scales, laid like roof tiles, and each scale is carved with ridges so precise they bend light, cancelling out every colour except blue. It's called structural colour. Grind the wing into powder and the blue disappears. Because the colour was never a pigment. It was a shape.

## #8 — THE SIX-NEEDLE BITE (MOSQUITO PROBOSCIS)
Number eight. A mosquito bite feels like one tiny prick. Under a microscope, it's surgery. That 'needle' is actually a sheath holding six separate needles. Two have serrated edges that saw through your skin. Two hold the tissue apart. One pumps in saliva that stops your blood from clotting. And the last one bends around inside your skin, hunting for a blood vessel. The itchy bump afterwards? That's your immune system reacting to the saliva it left behind.

## #7 — SKIN MADE OF TEETH (SHARK SKIN)
Number seven. Run your hand along a shark, and it feels smooth one way... and like sandpaper the other. That's because shark skin is covered in millions of microscopic teeth, called dermal denticles. They're built like real teeth, with a hard outer coating, dentine, and even a pulp cavity. And they guide water so efficiently that engineers copied them for racing swimsuits, and for hospital surfaces that bacteria struggle to stick to.

## #6 — FEET THAT DEFY GRAVITY (GECKO TOE PADS)
Number six. A gecko can sprint up a pane of glass and hang upside down by a single toe. No glue. No suction. Zoom in, and each foot is covered in hundreds of thousands of tiny hairs, and each hair splits into hundreds of even tinier tips. They get so close to the surface that molecular forces make them stick. In theory, if every hair grabbed at once, one gecko could hold up around 130 kilos. That's about the weight of two adults.

## #5 — YOUR BED'S HIDDEN ARMY (HOUSE DUST MITES)
Number five. Tonight, you'll share your bed with an army. Dust mites are too small to see, but a single mattress can be home to hundreds of thousands of them. They have no eyes. They breathe through their skin. And they eat the dead skin cells you shed every night. Here's the gross part: when you're allergic to dust, you're mostly not allergic to the mites themselves. You're allergic to their poop.

## #4 — MITES LIVING ON YOUR FACE (DEMODEX FOLLICULORUM)
Number four. And this one's personal. Right now, there are probably tiny mites living in the pores of your face. Almost every adult has them. They're called Demodex. They spend the day head-down inside your hair follicles, eating oil. At night, they crawl out onto your skin to find a mate... then crawl back in. For decades, people claimed they had no anus. In 2022, scientists proved that's a myth. And they're becoming so dependent on us, they may one day be unable to live anywhere else.

## #3 — THE UNKILLABLE ANIMAL (TARDIGRADE · 'WATER BEAR')
Meet the tardigrade, or 'water bear'. An eight-legged animal, most barely half a millimetre long. Dry it out, and it curls into a tiny ball and basically switches life off. In that state, it can survive temperatures close to absolute zero, and radiation hundreds of times stronger than what would kill you. In 2007, scientists sent tardigrades into orbit and exposed them to the vacuum of space. Some survived. Researchers in Japan even revived tardigrades that had been frozen for 30 years.

## #2 — THE ALIEN ROBOT (BACTERIOPHAGE)
Number two. This looks like a lunar lander. It's actually a virus called a bacteriophage, and it only hunts bacteria. It lands on its target, grips it with spindly legs, and injects its DNA like a syringe. Within about half an hour, the bacterium becomes a factory, building new phages until it bursts. There are an estimated ten million trillion trillion of them on Earth. More than every star in the observable universe. And doctors have started using them to save patients from superbugs that antibiotics can no longer kill.

## #1 — YOU (THE HUMAN BODY)
And number one... is you. Right now, inside your body, there are roughly 38 trillion bacteria. About as many as your own human cells. Every second, your bone marrow builds around two million new red blood cells. Your skin sheds tens of thousands of dead cells every minute: food for the mites in your bed. And on your face, Demodex are quietly waiting for nightfall. Under a microscope, there's no clear line where 'you' ends and everything else begins. The most alien world on this list... has been you all along.
